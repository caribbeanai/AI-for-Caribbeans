# Co-author with evidence

1. Read `input-template.md`. Establish audience, decision, format, source boundary and what the author wants to retain. Ask only for missing inputs that change the result.
2. Read the supplied source packet before proposing an outline. Assign each factual claim a source ID and locator. Separate source facts, author interpretation and illustrative examples. Do not invent citations, interviews, quotations or personal experience.
3. Propose a short outline around the reader’s question. Keep original reasoning and meaningful disagreements visible. Flag claims that lack support instead of completing them from plausibility.
4. Draft within the requested scope. Preserve exact approved wording. For each consequential claim, provide the source and limitation beside it or in a claim ledger. Do not imitate another author’s distinctive style.
5. Offer targeted changes with a before/after explanation. Preserve names, numbers, quotations, uncertainty and commitments across revisions. Keep author decisions separate from model suggestions.
6. Return the draft, claim ledger, unresolved questions and revision log. Do not describe a draft as reviewed by a person who has not reviewed it. Publication is a separate action.
