# Worked synthetic example

All people, figures and events below are invented teaching inputs. This is an authored expected response, not a live model trial.

Synthetic input: A neighbourhood library wants an internal note of up to 100 words on a four-week reminder pilot. Source A: 40 reminder messages were sent, one to each of 40 distinct recipients; 12 of those recipients returned books within seven days. Source B: the pilot had no comparison group. The author wants to propose a longer trial, not assert success.

Worked draft: “The library sent one reminder to each of 40 distinct recipients during its four-week pilot. Twelve recipients returned books within seven days. That is 30% of the people contacted, but it does not show how many would have returned books without a reminder. A longer trial with an agreed comparison could help us judge whether reminders change return behaviour. Staff should also record the time spent preparing messages and handling replies. The decision now is whether to design that trial, rather than treat the pilot as proof of effectiveness.”

Claim ledger: 40 distinct recipients, one message each, and 12 recipients returning books → A; 12/40 = 30% → arithmetic using A; no causal conclusion → B; longer trial and staff-time record → proposals, not findings.
Author decision: approve the next-trial proposal or remove it. Revision demonstration: the unsupported wording “reminders improved returns by 30%” would be replaced with a proportion and explicit causal limit.
