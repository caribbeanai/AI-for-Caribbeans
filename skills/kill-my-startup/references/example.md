# Teaching example

## Request
A Kingston repair shop wants to build a booking app. Ten people liked the idea; none paid. It proposes JMD 2,000 per booking, with JMD 800 variable costs. Produce a test before development.

## Response checks
Calculate JMD 1,200 contribution per booking before fixed costs. Treat likes as interest. Propose a real booking test with a stated owner-approved threshold and budget, without contacting customers.


## Completed decision brief
Decision: test bookings before building the app. Owner: the shop owner.
Evidence: ten positive comments; no paid bookings. Demand remains unverified.
Assumption to test: customers will book a repair at JMD 2,000 using a simple booking form.
Proposed test: invite ten eligible existing customers through an approved channel to book a real slot. Explain the service and cancellation terms before payment. Agree the budget and outreach permission first.
Proposed decision rule: proceed to a small delivery pilot if at least three book and the shop can deliver the work at the stated cost. Change the offer if customers decline for a repeated reason. This threshold is a teaching choice for owner approval, not a proven benchmark.
Contribution per booking: JMD 2,000 minus JMD 800 = JMD 1,200 before fixed costs. Three bookings would contribute JMD 3,600 before fixed costs. Check whether the JMD 800 already includes fees, labour and returns. No break-even claim is possible without fixed costs.
Next check: record invitations, bookings, reasons for refusal and actual delivery cost after the proposed one-week test. No invitations have been sent.
