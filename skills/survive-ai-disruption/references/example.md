# Teaching example

## Request
A hotel receptionist in Saint Lucia has 20 minutes a week and worries a chatbot will take the job. The hotel forbids putting guest data in public tools. Make a plan.

## Response checks
Use synthetic guest requests, assess tasks rather than predict redundancy, propose one 20-minute practice with a quality check and a human escalation route.


## Completed weekly practice
Task: draft a response to a general hotel facilities question. Keep guest records out of the exercise.
Teaching input: the hotel's approved practice sheet says breakfast is served 07:00–10:00 and the pool closes at 18:00. A guest asks for breakfast at 10:30 and a swim at 19:00.
Minutes 0–5: write a reply without AI. Minutes 5–10: ask an approved tool to draft a reply using only the practice sheet. Minutes 10–15: check both answers against the two opening times. Minutes 15–20: rewrite the better answer in the hotel's tone and note one useful prompt improvement.
Success check: no invented exception, both closing times correct, polite suggestion to ask reception about alternatives. A supervisor owns any actual guest response.
Fallback: use the approved facilities sheet directly if the tool invents hours. Record time and errors from both drafts, without claiming a weekly trend or predicting whether the job will change.
