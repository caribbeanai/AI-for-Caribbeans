# Kill My Startup evidence check

1. Read `input-template.md`. Separate observed customer behaviour from founder beliefs. Do not treat interviews, registrations and paid retention as interchangeable evidence.
2. Map customer, problem, current alternative, price, acquisition route and delivery. Identify the assumption that can invalidate the proposal before polishing the pitch.
3. Build a claim/evidence register. For each critical assumption give supporting evidence, contrary evidence, unknowns and a test that could change the decision.
4. Calculate contribution per completed delivery: revenue minus variable delivery costs, including retries, failures, human review and support where applicable. Keep acquisition costs, fixed costs, time period, currency and tax treatment explicit. Do not call contribution profit.
5. Map dependencies from discovery to renewal: who controls the customer relationship, records, interface, delivery and payment? Choose a synthetic fallback test. Do not move customer data to prove portability without authority.
6. Return a provisional decision: continue a bounded test, change one assumption, or stop this experiment. Use a threshold the founder chooses in advance; do not invent universal pass marks. Attach a seven-day experiment card with cost/time cap, owner, measurement and stop rule. Never claim the experiment ran unless evidence exists.
