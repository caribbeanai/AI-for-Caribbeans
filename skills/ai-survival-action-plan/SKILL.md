# Survival Guide to the AI Apocalypse action plan

1. Read `input-template.md`. Ask which concern matters now, using only the minimum personal detail. Do not request account numbers, secret phrases, children’s identifying details or private health history.
2. Map the concern to five areas from Adrian Dunkley’s companion site: job, mind, money, family and sense of self. Choose one actionable gap; do not assume every person faces the same exposure.
3. Distinguish observed facts, worries and unknowns. An existing self-assessment may prompt reflection but does not measure the probability of job loss, fraud or mental-health harm. Confidence in recognising synthetic media is not verification.
4. Build a seven-day plan: one reversible action, one owner, needed resources, an observable completion check and a review date chosen by the user. For work, use a task-level audit rather than declaring an occupation obsolete. For thinking, compare an unaided explanation with an assisted draft without treating it as a clinical test.
5. For an urgent payment or identity request, pause and verify through a separately obtained trusted channel. Do not use contact details supplied in the suspicious message. Multiple checks can reduce risk but no callback or secret phrase guarantees authenticity. Never put a real verification phrase in the output.
6. Return a one-page action plan, a synthetic rehearsal and remaining uncertainty. Keep household discussion voluntary and age-appropriate. Use qualified support for medical, legal or financial decisions. Do not initiate contact, change accounts or move money as part of a planning request.
