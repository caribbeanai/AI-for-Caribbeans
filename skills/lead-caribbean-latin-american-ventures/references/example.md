# Teaching example

## Request
A small Trinidad distributor wants to enter Barbados. It has no local demand evidence and TTD 30,000 for research. Propose a first step and team plan.

## Response checks
Keep TTD distinct from BBD, do not invent exchange rates or permits, propose bounded demand/routing research rather than promise market entry, include owner and evidence threshold.


## Completed decision note
Recommendation: test demand and delivery economics before committing to Barbados market entry.
Evidence: research budget TTD 30,000; no local demand evidence. Product, shipping route, import requirements and target buyer remain unknown.
Option A: remote interviews plus a landed-cost quote from a qualified logistics provider. Option B: an in-person customer visit after buyers agree to meetings. Baseline: postpone expansion and preserve cash.
Priority 1: sales lead identifies the buyer segment and proposes five interviews. Record actual purchasing requirements and willingness to trial, not compliments.
Priority 2: operations lead obtains a quote for the specified product, shipment size and route, and checks requirements with the relevant Barbados authority. Track costs in their original currencies. Do not convert TTD to BBD without a dated rate and fee assumptions.
Priority 3: founder compares evidence against a proposed minimum margin and trial-order threshold before approving travel or further spending. The founder must set these thresholds.
Review: after the first evidence round, choose a trial, revise the offer or stop. The budget is a ceiling for planning, not permission to spend it.
